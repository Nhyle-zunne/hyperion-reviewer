## 1. Morning cumulus

**Question:** If there are small cumulus clouds in the morning in summer, what kind of cloud cover would you expect and is the reasonable to forecast later in the day?

**Bank answer:** CB cloud.

Cumulonimbus (CB) is the answer used in this question. Summer heating can make warm, moist air rise and form cumulus clouds. If the atmosphere allows continued vertical growth, these clouds can develop into cumulonimbus, which can produce showers and thunderstorms. This describes development under suitable conditions; small cumulus does not always become CB.

**Remember:** Summer heating and suitable instability can build cumulus into CB.

## 2. Gyroscope apparent drift

**Question:** What do you call a gyroscope error that affects the heading system due to the rotation of the earth?

**Bank answer:** Apparent drift

Apparent drift results from the Earth rotating beneath a gyroscope whose axis tends to maintain its direction in space. To an observer on Earth, the gyro axis appears to change direction. The clue here is Earth’s rotation; transport effects instead involve moving the gyro over the Earth’s surface.

**Remember:** Earth’s rotation: apparent drift. Movement over Earth: transport effects.

## 3. Compass accuracy at high latitude

**Question:** In what area on the earth surface does the compass decreases its accuracy?

**Bank answer:** 75 degree latitude

The question bank uses 75° latitude for this item. The principle being tested is reduced compass reliability at high latitudes. A conventional gyrocompass has weaker north-seeking action toward the poles, while a magnetic compass becomes less dependable where the horizontal magnetic field is weak. Actual operating limits depend on the instrument.

**Remember:** For this bank item: 75°. The concept is high-latitude compass limitations.

## 4. Cold front

**Question:** What is your idea of a Cold Front?

**Bank answer:** It is the line of discontinuity at the surface or a upper level along which an advancing cold air mass is undercutting a warmer air mass.

A cold front forms where advancing colder, denser air moves beneath and replaces warmer air. The warm air is forced upward, which can produce clouds and precipitation when sufficient moisture is present. The key idea is the advancing cold air undercutting the warm air.

**Remember:** Cold front: cold air advances beneath warm air.

## 5. Chart courses and bearings

**Question:** How would you categorize the courses and the bearings which are laid down on the chart?

**Bank answer:** All the courses and all the bearings which are laid down down on the chart are true.

Courses and bearings plotted in ordinary nautical chart work are normally expressed as true directions, referenced to geographic north. Compass-observed bearings must therefore be converted to true before plotting. For a magnetic compass, this means allowing for both deviation and variation.

**Remember:** Plot true directions, measured from geographic north.

## 6. Jet stream

**Question:** Base on what you know, would you explain how the jet stream affect weather conditions around the world?

**Bank answer:** The strong wind current of the jet stream pushes weather systems around the world

The jet stream is a band of strong winds high in the atmosphere that helps steer weather systems. Changes in its position and path influence where weather systems move. That steering action explains the bank’s answer that the strong wind current pushes weather systems around the world.

**Remember:** Jet streams steer weather systems.

## 7. Current drift calculation

**Question:** At 1200H, you fix your position and change course to 315T. At 1225H, you again fix your position, and its 0.9 mile southwest of your DR position. Find the drift:

**Bank answer:** 2.1 knots

The elapsed time is 25 minutes, or 25/60 hour. Taking the distance as 0.9 nautical mile, drift = distance ÷ time = 0.9 ÷ (25/60) = 2.16 knots. The bank’s answer, 2.1 knots, is the closest listed option. The southwest displacement indicates the current’s set; drift is its speed.

**Remember:** Drift = nautical miles × 60 ÷ minutes; choose the closest listed answer.

## 8. Polaris and north

**Question:** Polaris always appears due north in the sky to a precision better than how many degree?

**Bank answer:** one degree

The bank uses one degree as the answer for Polaris as an approximate north reference. Polaris currently lies less than one degree from the north celestial pole, making it useful for finding north. Its precise bearing still varies with the observer’s latitude and time; an accurate bearing requires a Polaris azimuth correction.

**Remember:** One degree is the bank’s approximate Polaris answer; precise bearings need correction.

## 9. IALA Region B starboard mark

**Question:** What is the color of the Starboard hand lateral marks used in IALA Region B?

**Bank answer:** Red

In IALA Region B, starboard-hand lateral marks are red. When following the conventional direction of buoyage, normally entering a harbour from seaward, these marks are kept to starboard. Check the chart’s direction of buoyage when applying this rule.

**Remember:** Region B: red, right, returning—check the direction of buoyage.

## 10. Aligned Sun, Moon and Earth

**Question:** What conclusion can you draw if the moon and sun align with the Earth?

**Bank answer:** The tides will be stronger because the attraction of the moon and sun is combine

When the Sun, Moon and Earth align, the solar and lunar tidal effects reinforce each other. This produces spring tides, with a larger difference between high and low water. That increased tidal range is the meaning of “stronger” in the bank’s answer.

**Remember:** Aligned Sun and Moon reinforce tidal range: spring tides.
